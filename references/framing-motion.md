# Reference: Framing & Video Motion Dictionary

## Camera Framing & Angles (Use in Start Frame Prompts)
- **Extreme Wide Shot (EWS):** Establishes geography, weather, and scale of environment.
- **Wide Shot (WS):** Full body of character within surrounding setting.
- **Medium Shot (MS):** Waist-up framing; standard for physical actions and interaction.
- **Medium Close-Up (MCU):** Chest-up framing; captures subtle facial micro-expressions.
- **Extreme Close-Up (ECU) / Insert:** Tight focus on eyes, hands, tools, or symbolic props.
- **Over-The-Shoulder (OTS):** Framed past one character's shoulder toward another subject or object.
- **High-Angle Candid:** Elevated downward perspective creating vulnerability or observational distance.
- **Low-Angle Hero:** Upward perspective adding stature or dramatic sky framing.

## Camera Movements (Use ONLY in Video Motion Prompts)
- `Static locked-off tripod shot` (best for atmospheric dialogue and clean character consistency)
- `Slow, subtle dolly-in toward subject`
- `Gentle horizontal pan right / left`
- `Slow crane down revealing foreground`
- `Subtle handheld documentary micro-movement`

## Environmental Micro-Motion (Use ONLY in Video Motion Prompts)
- `Gentle wind swaying loose hair strands and fabric`
- `Visible cold breath vapor drifting slowly in chilly air`
- `Soft rain drizzle creating ripples on wet asphalt`
- `Floating dust motes catching warm sunbeams`
- `Distant background commuters or train lights moving softly out of focus`

## Standard Cinematic Sequencing & Grammar
1. **Shot Progression Rule:** Open new scenes with an `EWS` or `WS` (Establishing Shot) to orient the viewer spatially, step into `MS` for physical interaction, use `MCU` for emotional dialogue, and cut to `ECU / Insert` for key narrative props.
2. **180-Degree Rule (Dialogue Blocking):** In two-person conversations (`OTS` or `Shot-Reverse-Shot`), keep the camera on one side of the imaginary axis between characters. If Character A looks screen-right, Character B must look screen-left.
3. **Rule of Thirds & Headroom:** Place eyes on the upper-third line for `MS` and `MCU`. Leave directional look-room (lead room) in the direction a character is gazing or moving.
4. **Motion Discipline:** Limit each clip to ONE primary camera movement OR ONE primary subject action. Never combine fast camera pans with complex multi-character movement in a single 5-second clip.
